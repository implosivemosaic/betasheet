# Example: capture an offering, then check its details

Hogtown's November 2026 refresh shows why calendar dates and times must both be checked. Its Intro to
Bouldering calendar still offered the same 42 dates, but November 14 had one slot at 10–11:30 am,
instead of the stored 3–4:30 pm. Sampling another Saturday would miss the change. The published
update retained every date and changed only that time.

## A useful gym handoff

1. Open the homepage and relevant programme/event pages. Save raw text and follow linked booking
   offerings, including new links missing from the source registry.
2. Capture confirmed dates and their times. A course booking may expose its complete Day #1–#N
   sequence in one click; a drop-in calendar may require individual date reads. Preserve holiday
   gaps. Mark partial windows explicitly; keep unknown times null rather than copy a sampled weekday.
3. Inspect relevant posters and expand social captions. Save raw images/text plus the source URL.
   A saved unread image remains a follow-up, not completed research.
4. Scroll Facebook before deciding a feed is empty. Instagram pins require reading several candidate
   posts and their own publication dates. Follow relevant bio/registration links.
5. Compare with the last successful source capture and current catalogue. Keep evidence, candidates,
   held findings and coverage separate. A source failure preserves the previous successful checkpoint.

## Example evidence index

| Finding | Evidence | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Hogtown Intro, November 14 | Individually read booking slot, 10–11:30 am | Propose one time correction; preserve other sessions |
| RGP course with Day #1–#3 | All three dates and times returned by the offering | Record that exact sequence, including any gaps |
| Empty booking widget | Page shell captured, calendar not rendered | Partial check; retry once, then follow up |
| OCF #303 | Index matches; event page publishes proposed category times | Capture both separately and propose detail changes |

For OCF, a matching index is the start of the detail check. The shared collector reads every upcoming
event page and records a coverage row. Source ownership may be shared; event details still need their
own evidence.

## Tools

Run public browser sessions in independently owned playground containers. Connect authenticated
lookups with `lego browser connect m3-ghost/browser`; take the shared lock briefly per gym, open your
own tabs, read and close them. Reconnect once on a transport failure. Record unresolved access as a
specific coverage gap and keep working. The coordinator owns the final combined validation and report.
